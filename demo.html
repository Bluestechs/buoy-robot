Proposed by the kids

They imagined a way to help a lake. We helped picture their idea.

They did the work. These are their original problem and proposed solutions, in their own words. If they had more time, it might have gotten here. They might do it differently next time.

The Problem

The Biodiversity problem we chose was algae blooms. Specifically, how to minimize and kill problematic algae. Fertilizer, like phosphorus and nitrogen from farms, will get into runoff water when big rains happen and will flow into the nearest lake or river, which will fertilize the algae. The only reason this is a problem is that algae will grow faster than the natural predators of algae can eat it in the habitat. If it gets big enough, the algae will block sunlight, killing the plants, which then kills the animals that eat the plants, etc. This limits biodiversity and hurts the overall habitat.

The Solutions

The solution we decided on is for a levee to surround the edge of a farm to catch and absorb the water runoff with fertilizer. We also have a system where every year a boat would drive around a lake or an inlet and release beneficial bacteria, which will eat the phosphorus and nitrogen fertilizer and essentially starve the algae. But when an especially hard rain comes, there would be a buoy. In the buoy, there would be two tanks: one of them holds a sodium peroxyhydrate solution that, when mixed with water, can kill algae; the other is filled with water and a one-way valve. The water tank would lead to a sprayer on the side that would spray the water and spread the sodium peroxyhydrate on top. On the top of the buoy, there would be solar panels that would charge the battery inside the buoy. The battery would then power a transmitter, small CPU, a water filter, and an antenna. The antenna would connect to the weather network in the nearby area, and when the weather predicted heavy rain, it would tell the CPU to open the valve and mix the water, or when the water sensor on the bottom picked up an overabundance of algae in the water. When all of these are combined, we hope that the algae will die and the lake will return to its pre-algae self.
